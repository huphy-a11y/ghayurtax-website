Why reconciliation surprises happen

Your allocation is an estimate. The reconciliation is the bill.

Under cost-based funding, a centre's CWELCC allocation is the Region's estimate of what the program should cost to run. After year-end the Region reconciles that estimate against what you actually spent — site by site — and recovers the difference wherever spending came in below the allocation or outside the eligible-cost rules. If costs ran over, the shortfall is yours to absorb.

Where centres get caught

  • Ineligible or unattributable costs. Marketing, amounts treated as being in lieu of profit (such as an owner's year-end bonus), vehicle costs, donations, tuition reimbursements, and costs for children outside CWELCC that weren't tracked separately.
  • Base-fee revenue. Reconciled at the higher of expected or actual. The 2026 formula assumes a 5% vacancy rate, so fees that weren't collected aren't made up by funding.
  • Wage rules. RECE wage floors ($25.86 for program staff and $26.86 for supervisors in 2026), the GOF → WEG → WCF order of operations, enhancements paid on every cheque and shown separately on pay stubs.
  • Planning time and professional learning. Four paid hours a month and up to $500 per full-time equivalent, with staff notices and proof to the Region due on fixed dates early in the year.
  • Multi-site operators. Since 2026 each site reconciles on its own. A surplus at one location can't cover ineligible costs at another.
  • Year-end paperwork. Audited financial statements and the Financial Annual Information Return by April 30 in Peel; Standardized Financial Reports and audited statements within four months of year-end in Toronto.

None of this is hard to get right with books that were set up for it. It is hard to fix in March from a shoebox.

What we do — and don't

Audited financial statements must be issued by a Licensed Public Accountant. We don't audit. We keep your books, prepare the reconciliation and the funding reports, and hand your auditor a clean, tied-out file.

Because an auditor can't also do your bookkeeping, most centres need both — and the bookkeeping side is where the reconciliation is won or lost.

A year with GATS

The funding year has a rhythm. We keep you ahead of it.

Dates below follow Peel's 2026 guideline; Halton and Toronto set their own. We track the ones that apply to you.

  1. January

    Set the year up

    Staff notices for planning time and professional learning go out (by January 30 in Peel, proof by February 13). Wage floors and enhancement stacking confirmed for the new year. Spending plan set against your allocation.

  2. February – April

    Close the prior year

    T4s and payroll reconciliations by the end of February. Books closed, reconciliation package and FAIR or SFR prepared, audited statements to the Region by April 30 in Peel.

  3. May – September

    Check the run-rate

    Quarterly checks of spending against allocation and base-fee revenue against expected, so a shortfall or an overspend shows up with months left to act.

  4. October – December

    Plan the finish

    Eligible spending that still fits the allocation, professional learning budget used, top-ups reviewed and next year's staffing costs modelled. December 31 is the last day this year's eligible costs count.

Common questions

What operators ask us first.

Do you do the audit?

No. Audited financial statements have to be issued by a Licensed Public Accountant, and the auditor can't also keep your books. We do the bookkeeping, payroll, reconciliation and tax, and give your auditor a file that ties out — which is what keeps the audit short.

Can you take over part-way through the year?

Yes. We rebuild the year-to-date books with eligible-cost coding, reconcile the payroll enhancements paid so far, and give you a mid-year position against the allocation before anything else.

We run more than one site. Does that change things?

Since 2026 each site reconciles on its own and funding can't move between sites, so we keep site-level books and a site-level spending plan. Shared costs are allocated on a documented basis the Region can follow.

We're a non-profit centre. Are we a fit?

Yes. Non-profit centres reconcile the same way; the differences are the surplus rules and filing a T1044 or T3010 instead of a T2. We handle both.

What does it cost?

It depends on the number of sites and the state of the books. Call or email and we'll quote after a short look at your last reconciliation.

Which Regions do you work with?

Peel, Halton and Toronto, and other Ontario service system managers on request. The provincial guideline is the same everywhere; the reporting templates and dates differ, and we track them.
