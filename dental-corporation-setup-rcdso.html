Setting up a dentistry professional corporation: requirements, naming and RCDSO steps.

Incorporating is only half the job. An Ontario dentist has to incorporate a corporation that already meets the College's rules, give it a name in a prescribed format, and then obtain an RCDSO Certificate of Authorization before the corporation can practise. Here is the whole sequence.

Step 1 — the requirements

What the corporation must look like before you apply.

The health profession corporation rules under Ontario Regulation 39/02 apply to dentists as they do to other regulated health professionals. These conditions must already be true on the day you apply, so build them into the articles at incorporation.

  • Restricted objects. The articles must state that the corporation cannot carry on any business other than the practice of dentistry and activities related or ancillary to it.
  • Voting shares. Held by dentists who are members of the RCDSO.
  • Non-voting shares. May be held by College members, by a family member of a voting dentist shareholder (spouse, child or parent), or by a trust for the dentist’s minor children with those children as beneficiaries.
  • Directors and officers. Must be dentists who are members of the College and authorized to sign for the corporation.
  • Jurisdiction. Ontario only. The RCDSO does not permit a federally incorporated dentistry professional corporation.

Set the share classes at incorporation: voting shares for the dentist, a separate non-voting class for a spouse or a family trust. Dividends to family are limited by the income-splitting rules until age 65, but amending share structure later costs more than getting it right once.

Step 2 — the name

The format is prescribed:

Surname of one or more dentist shareholders as it appears on the College register, optionally with a given name or initials — then Dentistry Professional Corporation (or the French equivalent).

Example: Jane A. Smith Dentistry Professional Corporation.

The name must indicate the profession, must include the words Professional Corporation, and must not carry any other information — your practice’s trade name cannot form part of it. Numbered companies are not permitted. The RCDSO publishes its own naming criteria; check it before the articles are filed, because a rejected name means filing again.

Step 3 — the RCDSO application

What the College needs, in order.

The corporation exists once the articles are filed, but it cannot practise dentistry until the Certificate of Authorization is issued.

  1. Incorporate under the Ontario Business Corporations Act

    In the prescribed name, with the restricted objects and the share classes above. Keep the certified copy of the certificate of incorporation and of any later endorsed certificates.

  2. Order a certificate of status

    Dated no more than 30 days before you submit, so leave it until the rest of the package is ready.

  3. Apply to the RCDSO and pay $750

    Non-refundable. A revised certificate later — after a name change, for example — costs $150.

  4. Provide the declaration and the disclosure

    A director’s statutory declaration that the corporation complies with section 3.2 of the Business Corporations Act and carries on no business other than dentistry and ancillary activities, together with the shareholders, directors, officers and the address of the practice.

  5. Then open the CRA and bank accounts

    Corporate tax and payroll accounts, a corporate bank account, and a clean start date for moving billings and staff payroll across. Most dental services are HST-exempt, so an HST account is usually not needed — check if you do cosmetic work.

  6. Tell the parties who pay you

    Principal dentists, associates and any plans or assignment arrangements need the corporation’s details from the start date, and the associate agreement may need to be assigned to the corporation.

Step 4 — afterwards

Keeping the certificate.

  • Renew every year by August 31. Every RCDSO certificate expires on August 31 whatever date it was issued, and a certificate that is not renewed is revoked effective September 1. Diarize July, not August.
  • Report changes. Shareholder, director, officer, name and premises changes go to the College, with a fresh declaration where required.
  • Keep the minute book current. Annual resolutions, share registers and the Ontario annual return.
  • Keep the corporation clean. Personal expenses run through the practice are the most common problem we see on a first year-end.

Quick answers

What dentists ask about setting up a corporation.

What can I name my dentistry professional corporation?

The surname of one or more dentist shareholders as it appears on the College register, optionally with a given name or initials, followed by “Dentistry Professional Corporation”. Your practice trade name cannot be part of it, and numbered companies are not permitted.

Can I practise through the corporation as soon as it is incorporated?

No. The corporation needs an RCDSO Certificate of Authorization before it can practise dentistry.

What does the RCDSO charge?

$750 for the initial application and $150 for a revised certificate. Certificates expire every August 31 and must be renewed annually; one that is not renewed is revoked on September 1.

Can I federally incorporate my dental practice?

No. The RCDSO requires incorporation under the Ontario Business Corporations Act.

Can my spouse be a director of my dentistry professional corporation?

No. Directors and officers must be dentists who are members of the College. A spouse can hold non-voting shares, and a trust can hold them for your minor children.
