CWELCC guide · 2026 guidelines

The CWELCC reconciliation, step by step.

Your CWELCC allocation is an estimate; the reconciliation is the bill. After December 31 the Region compares what it funded with what you actually spent, site by site, and recovers the difference. Here is how that comparison works and where centres lose money.

What gets reconciled

Five pieces, each tested on its own.

PieceHow the Region looks at it
Program cost allocationActual eligible costs against the allocation, for each licensed site on its own
Base-fee revenueThe higher of expected or actual. The 2026 formula assumes a 5% vacancy rate, so uncollected fees are not made up by funding
Workforce fundingWage floors, Wage Enhancement Grant (WEG) and workforce compensation (WCF) paid to eligible staff, in the right order, on every pay
Planning time and professional learningFour paid hours a month and up to $500 per full-time equivalent, with staff notices and proof on file
In lieu of profit or surplusThe allowance set by the formula. Extra amounts taken out, such as an owner's year-end bonus, are treated as ineligible

The six steps

From December 31 to the Region's package.

  1. Close the books to December 31

    Bank and credit card accounts reconciled, payroll agreed to the T4 summary, accruals booked. December 31 is the last day a cost counts for the year.

  2. Sort every cost: eligible, ineligible, or not CWELCC

    Marketing, donations, vehicle costs, tuition reimbursements and amounts in lieu of profit are the usual ineligible items. Costs for children outside CWELCC, such as school-age programs, must be tracked separately or they cannot be attributed.

  3. Split by site

    Since 2026 each site reconciles alone. A surplus at one location cannot cover an overspend or ineligible costs at another. Shared costs such as head-office salaries need an allocation method you can explain and repeat.

  4. Test base-fee revenue

    Compare fees actually collected with the expected revenue in your allocation. If actual is lower, the Region still uses expected.

  5. Prove the workforce money

    For each eligible employee: hours, base wage, WEG, workforce compensation and the floor top-up, shown separately on pay stubs and paid every cheque, not as a year-end lump sum. See 2026 wage floors, WEG and WCF.

  6. Assemble the package

    The reconciliation schedules, the Region's annual return (the FAIR in Peel, Standardized Financial Reports in Toronto) and audited financial statements issued by a licensed public accountant. Dates are in CWELCC deadlines.

Where centres get caught

Six ways a recovery starts.

  • Coding after the fact. Sorting eligible from ineligible in March, from a year of uncoded transactions, is where most recoveries start.
  • Underspending quietly. Spending below the allocation is recovered. A quarterly check shows the gap while there are still months to use it on eligible costs.
  • Owner compensation. A salary for work performed is an eligible cost within the benchmarks. A discretionary bonus at year-end is treated as profit.
  • One set of books for several sites. Without site-level records, the multi-site rule cannot be met.
  • Closure days. Since January 1, 2026 every centre is held to a maximum of 20 planned paid closure days, no more than two weeks in a row or four weeks in a year; the transition for legacy centres ended December 31, 2025. A centre that had fewer closure days cannot simply raise them: tell the Region in writing first, because the change can reduce funding.
  • Budgets built from stale books. The operating budget you submit to the Region is only as good as the books behind it. Have your accountant keep the books current through the year, so the budget is accurate at submission and review and a recovery never comes as a surprise.

What audit-ready books look like

Your audited statements must come from a licensed public accountant, and an auditor cannot also keep your books. The bookkeeping side is where the reconciliation is won: monthly bank reconciliations, funding recorded by stream, payroll enhancements traceable to each employee, and a file that ties to the Region's reports.

Quick answers

What operators ask about the reconciliation.

When is the CWELCC reconciliation due?

In Peel, audited financial statements and the FAIR are due April 30. In Toronto, audited statements and Standardized Financial Reports are due within four months of year-end. Halton sets its own dates each year.

Can the Region take CWELCC money back?

Yes. Unspent allocation, ineligible costs and workforce funding that was not paid out as required are recovered after the reconciliation.

Does a non-profit centre reconcile differently?

The same cost rules apply. The allowance is a surplus allowance instead of profit, and the year-end return is a T1044 or T3010 instead of a T2.

Who can prepare a CWELCC reconciliation?

Your accountant or bookkeeper prepares it from the books; the audited statements that go with it must be issued by a licensed public accountant. GATS prepares reconciliations for centres and home child care agencies in Peel, Halton and Toronto and coordinates the audit.
