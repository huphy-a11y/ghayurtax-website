Dentist guide · 2026

Dentistry professional corporations in Ontario.

A dentistry professional corporation lets a dentist leave income in a company taxed at about 11.2%, against a top personal rate of 53.53%, and bring family in as non-voting shareholders. The RCDSO sets the rules on who can own and run it. Here is how it works and when it pays.

The RCDSO rules

Who can own and run the corporation.

  • Voting shares: dentists who are members of the College.
  • Non-voting shares: College members, a voting dentist's spouse, child or parent, or a trust for the dentist's minor children.
  • Directors and officers: dentists who are members of the College.
  • Incorporation: under the Ontario Business Corporations Act. Federal incorporation is not permitted.
  • Business: restricted to the practice of dentistry and activities related or ancillary to it.
  • Certificate of Authorization: $750 to apply. Every certificate expires on August 31 and must be renewed each year, whatever date it was issued.

When it pays

The corporation defers tax on income you do not need to spend. Associates with modest surplus and heavy student debt often wait; practice owners with equipment loans, staff and steady profit usually benefit early. Dividends to family members are limited by the income-splitting rules until you turn 65, the same as for physicians.

Quick answers

What dentists ask about incorporating.

Can my spouse own shares in my dentistry professional corporation?

Yes. A voting dentist's spouse, children and parents can hold non-voting shares, and a trust can hold them for minor children. Voting shares, directorships and officer roles are limited to dentists.

What does the RCDSO charge for a Certificate of Authorization?

$750 for the initial application. Certificates expire on August 31 every year and must be renewed annually.

Can a dentistry professional corporation be federally incorporated?

No. The RCDSO requires incorporation under the Ontario Business Corporations Act.

Should an associate dentist incorporate?

Only once there is income left over after living costs and debt payments, and provided the associate is genuinely an independent contractor. See our guide for associates.
