Home child care agencies: why high occupancy can mean a base-fee recovery.

A home child care agency that runs fuller than 95% should expect part of its CWELCC funding to be recovered. The formula assumes 5% vacancy. When homes are fuller than that, parents pay more base fees than the formula expected, and the Region reconciles on the higher number.

How the offset works

Three steps in the formula.

  1. Estimate base-fee revenue

    Active home seats for eligible children × the daily base fee × service days.

  2. Multiply by 0.95

    The result is the expected base-fee revenue offset, which is subtracted from your benchmark allocation.

  3. Reconcile on the higher number

    After year-end, funding is reconciled against the higher of expected or actual base-fee revenue.

What that means at 97% occupancy

AmountLine
$1,000,000Base fees at full capacity (illustration)
$950,000Expected offset at 95%

$20,000Funding recovered, all else equal

Round numbers, to show the mechanism. Your own figures depend on seats, fees and service days. Running below 95% does not work in reverse: the Region still uses the expected amount, and the agency absorbs the shortfall.

What an agency should do

Plan for the recovery before the letter arrives.

  • Track seats and fees monthly

    Active home seats and fee revenue, by home, against the numbers in the allocation tool.

  • Set the recovery aside

    If occupancy is running above 95%, accrue the likely recovery in the books each month so the cash is there in the spring.

  • Keep compensation streams separate

    Provider compensation, visitor compensation and agency operations are benchmarked separately, so record them that way.

  • Update the Region when homes change

    Homes opening or closing mid-year change both the allocation and the offset.

  • Keep the books current

    Have your accountant prepare the books through the year, so the operating budget you submit is accurate at review and the reconciliation holds no surprises.

  • Keep the file audit-ready

    Funding by stream, fees by home and payroll that ties out make the audit short. We coordinate it with our auditor.

Quick answers

What agencies ask us.

Why is our home child care agency facing a CWELCC recovery when we were full all year?

Because the formula assumes 5% vacancy. If your homes ran above 95% occupancy, actual base-fee revenue was higher than the expected offset, the Region reconciles on the higher number, and the difference comes back as a recovery.

Is the base-fee offset different for agencies and centres?

The 0.95 multiplier is the same. Centres are measured on operating spaces; agencies are measured on active home seats.

Does GATS work with home child care agencies?

Yes. GATS prepares CWELCC reconciliations for several licensed home child care agencies, as well as centres in Peel, Halton and Toronto, keeps agency books by home and by funding stream, and coordinates the audit with an independent licensed auditor.
