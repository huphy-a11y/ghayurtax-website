Start here

Grant, contribution, loan or credit — they're not the same thing.

  • Grant — money you don't repay. Usually a cash award or an in-kind service (free equipment, free training). Taxable as income or netted against the cost it funded.
  • Non-repayable contribution — also not repaid, but paid as a share of costs you've already incurred (typically 50–80%), against receipts. Cash flow matters: you spend first.
  • Loan — repaid, but cheaper or easier to get than a bank loan because a government guarantees or funds it.
  • Tax credit — claimed on the T2 or T1. A refundable credit is paid out even if no tax is owing; a non-refundable one only reduces tax. Filed late, it's gone: most have an 18-month clock.

What changed in 2026

Six things worth a phone call this year.

  • SR&ED got bigger

    The enhanced 35% refundable credit now applies to up to $6 million of eligible spending a year (was $3 million), and equipment bought for R&D qualifies again. Law since March 26, 2026.

  • 100% first-year write-off for equipment

    The federal Productivity Mega Deduction announced September 15, 2026 lets most machinery, computers, clean-energy equipment and zero-emission vehicles bought on or after that date be expensed in full in year one. Draft legislation — timing of purchases matters.

  • Ontario Made Manufacturing credit is 15%

    Up from 10%: a refundable 15% on new manufacturing buildings and machinery, to a maximum of $3 million a year, for investments after May 14, 2025. Runs to the end of 2029.

  • Ontario small-business tax rate cut

    From 3.2% to 2.2% on July 1, 2026 — about 11.2% combined on the first $500,000. The dividend tax credit on non-eligible dividends falls January 1, 2027, so salary-versus-dividend planning for 2026 is worth revisiting.

  • Ontario Job Grant is back

    The Canada-Ontario Job Grant paused in November 2025 and relaunched May 1, 2026 as the Ontario Job Grant: up to $10,000 per trainee, with small employers paying one-sixth.

  • New-home HST rebate for first-time buyers

    Federal GST rebate up to $50,000 plus Ontario's 8% part up to $80,000 — up to $130,000 on a new home up to $1 million. Ontario applications opened June 5, 2026.

Manufacturers

For manufacturers, the credits are worth more than the grants.

A Mississauga machine shop that bought $500,000 of equipment in 2026 can claim a $75,000 refundable Ontario credit, write the whole purchase off in year one, and — if it solved a technical problem along the way — claim SR&ED on the wages. None of it is automatic; all of it goes through the T2.

What a manufacturing file looks like with us

Books that track cost of goods, inventory and work-in-progress properly; payroll coded by project so SR&ED wages are provable; a capital-asset register with available-for-use dates for the Ontario Made credit and the Mega Deduction; and a T2 with Schedules 31, 508, 566 and 572 done before the deadline, not after.

Grant applications

For IRAP, FedDev, the Trade Fund and CanExport we prepare the financial package — three years of statements, the project budget, cash-flow projections and the tariff-impact analysis where it's asked for — and the claims and milestone reports afterwards. Ask before the project starts: most programs will not fund costs incurred before approval.

Common questions

What clients ask about grants and credits.

Is a grant taxable?

Usually, yes. A grant or wage subsidy is income to the business in the year it's received, or it reduces the cost of the asset it paid for. Refundable tax credits like SR&ED and the Ontario Made credit reduce the expense or asset they relate to. We account for it so there's no surprise at year-end — and it's still worth far more than not applying.

Can a sole proprietor apply, or do I need a corporation?

It depends on the program. IRAP, CanExport, SR&ED's 35% refundable rate and the Ontario Made credit are for corporations. Wage subsidies, the Ontario Job Grant, Save on Energy and most loans are open to sole proprietors. If a program you want requires a corporation, incorporating first is often the right sequence — see our incorporation page.

How long does it take to get the money?

Tax credits arrive with your assessment, typically two to four months after the T2 is filed. Wage subsidies pay after the placement, against payroll records. Contribution programs like IRAP and FedDev pay as you submit claims during the project, and a first payment can take a few months from approval. Nothing pays for costs you incurred before approval, so timing the application comes first.
